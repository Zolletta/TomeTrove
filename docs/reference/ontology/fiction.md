# Fiction

Prose narrative of any length (novel, short story, novella, fairy tale). The distinction between forms is ontological, not functional.

## Genre hierarchies

| Classification                        | Description                                                                                             | Examples                                                                                                               |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `Fiction/Adventure/Exploration`       | Expeditions, discoveries, uncharted territories                                                         | *The Lost World* (Conan Doyle), *Into the Wild* (Jon Krakauer)                                                         |
| `Fiction/Adventure/History`           | Adventure set in a historical period                                                                    | *The Three Musketeers* (Dumas), *Sharpe* (Bernard Cornwell)                                                            |
| `Fiction/Adventure/Sea`               | Naval adventure, piracy, storms                                                                         | *Treasure Island* (R.L. Stevenson), *Master and Commander* (Patrick O'Brian)                                           |
| `Fiction/Adventure/Survival`          | Struggle against nature, isolation                                                                      | *Hatchet* (Gary Paulsen), *The Revenant* (Michael Punke)                                                               |
| `Fiction/Adventure/Treasure`          | Treasure hunts, artifacts, relics                                                                       | *Raiders of the Lost Ark* (Campbell Black), *The Da Vinci Code* (Dan Brown)                                            |
| `Fiction/Experimentation/Language`    | Language-level experiments (Oulipo, constraint-based writing)                                           | *La Disparition* (Georges Perec), *Ulysses* (James Joyce)                                                              |
| `Fiction/Experimentation/Satire`      | Satirical experimentation, playful subversion of literary conventions                                   | *Gravity's Rainbow* (Pynchon), *Snow White* (Barthelme)                                                                |
| `Fiction/Experimentation/Structure`   | Narrative structure experiments, meta-fiction                                                           | *If on a winter's night a traveler* (Calvino), *House of Leaves* (Danielewski)                                         |
| `Fiction/Experimentation/Surrealism`  | Dream logic, automatic writing, juxtaposition                                                           | *The Trial* (Kafka), *The Master and Margarita* (Bulgakov)                                                             |
| `Fiction/Fantasy/Adventure`           | Sword & sorcery, low fantasy with quest-driven plot, focus on action                                    | *Glory Road* (Robert Heinlein)                                                                                         |
| `Fiction/Fantasy/Children`            | Fairy tales, fables and children's fiction                                                              | *Aesop's Fables* (Aesop), *The Adventures of Pinocchio* (C. Collodi)                                                   |
| `Fiction/Fantasy/Epic`                | High fantasy, secondary world, complex world-building, chivalric sagas                                  | *The Black Sword Triumphant* (M. Weis, T. Hickman), *The Lord of the Rings* (J.R.R. Tolkien)                           |
| `Fiction/Fantasy/Myth`                | Narrative reworking of ancient myths                                                                    | *Circe* (Madeline Miller), *Antigone* (Vittorio Alfieri)                                                               |
| `Fiction/Fantasy/Urban`               | Contemporary/urban setting, low fantasy where magic intrudes into the real world                        | *American Gods* (Neil Gaiman)                                                                                          |
| `Fiction/Horror/Body`                 | Body horror, physical transformation                                                                    | *The Fly* (Alan Dean Foster), *Blood Music* (Greg Bear)                                                                |
| `Fiction/Horror/Creature`             | Zombies, vampires, demons, monsters, epidemics                                                          | *World War Z* (Max Brooks), *The Thing* (Alan Dean Foster), *The Exorcist* (William Peter Blatty)                      |
| `Fiction/Horror/Folk`                 | Rural and folkloric horror, communal dread, pagan rituals                                               | *Harvest Home* (Thomas Tryon), *The Ritual* (Adam Nevill)                                                              |
| `Fiction/Horror/Gothic`               | Castles, atmospheres, dark romanticism, cosmic dread                                                    | *Dracula* (Bram Stoker), *The Fall of the House of Usher* (Edgar Allan Poe), *At the Mountains of Madness* (Lovecraft) |
| `Fiction/Horror/Psychology`           | Psychological horror, mental unease                                                                     | *The Haunting of Hill House* (Shirley Jackson), *Rosemary's Baby* (Ira Levin)                                          |
| `Fiction/Horror/Slasher`              | Serial killers, gore                                                                                    | *Halloween* (Curtis Richards), *Friday the 13th* (Simon Hawke)                                                         |
| `Fiction/Humor/Absurd`                | Absurd humor, surreal comedy, nonsense                                                                  | *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* (Douglas Adams), *At Swim-Two-Birds* (Flann O'Brien)                            |
| `Fiction/Humor/Children`              | Humor aimed at children                                                                                 | *Captain Underpants* (Dav Pilkey), *Diary of a Wimpy Kid* (Jeff Kinney)                                                |
| `Fiction/Humor/Parody`                | Parody of genres, authors, or styles                                                                    | *Bored of the Rings* (Harvard Lampoon), *Pride and Prejudice and Zombies* (Seth Graham-Smith)                          |
| `Fiction/Humor/Satire`                | Social, political, or institutional satire                                                              | *Catch-22* (Joseph Heller), *Animal Farm* (George Orwell)                                                              |
| `Fiction/Magical Realism`             | Pure genre, no subcategories. Magic placed in an otherwise real and everyday world without explanations | *One Hundred Years of Solitude* (Marquez), *The House of the Spirits* (Allende)                                        |
| `Fiction/Mystery/History`             | Mysteries set in past eras                                                                              | *Aristotle Detective* (Margaret Doody)                                                                                 |
| `Fiction/Mystery/Humor`               | Mysteries with a bright or satirical tone                                                               | *Too Many Cooks* (Rex Stout), *A Cantare Fu Il Cane* (Andrea Vitali)                                                   |
| `Fiction/Mystery/Investigation`       | The classic "Whodunnit"                                                                                 | *Murder on the Orient Express* (Agatha Christie), *A is for Alibi* (Sue Grafton)                                       |
| `Fiction/Romance/Death`               | Tragic love, loss, grief                                                                                | *Romeo and Juliet* (Shakespeare), *A Little Life* (Hanya Yanagihara)                                                   |
| `Fiction/Romance/Destiny`             | Love driven by fate, inevitable encounter                                                               | *The Time Traveler's Wife* (Audrey Niffenegger), *One Day* (David Nicholls)                                            |
| `Fiction/Romance/History`             | Historical romance, period context                                                                      | *Outlander* (Diana Gabaldon), *The Bronze Horseman* (Paullina Simons)                                                  |
| `Fiction/Romance/Memory`              | Love in memory, nostalgia, longing                                                                      | *Call Me by Your Name* (Andre Aciman), *Persuasion* (Jane Austen)                                                      |
| `Fiction/Romance/Second Chance`       | Reunion, reconciliation                                                                                 | *One True Loves* (Taylor Jenkins Reid), *It Ends with Us* (Colleen Hoover)                                             |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Alien`       | First contact, invasion                                                                                 | *The War of the Worlds* (H.G. Wells)                                                                                   |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Cyberpunk`   | High tech, low life, biotechnologies, networks                                                          | *Neuromancer* (William Gibson)                                                                                         |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Dystopia`    | Oppressive or post-apocalyptic societies                                                                | *A Scanner Darkly* (P.K. Dick), *1984* (G. Orwell)                                                                     |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Hard Sci-Fi` | Focus on scientific and technological rigor                                                             | *The Martian* (Andy Weir)                                                                                              |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Space`       | Space opera, planetary exploration, galactic empires                                                    | *2001: A Space Odyssey* (A.C. Clarke)                                                                                  |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Time`        | Time travel, paradoxes, temporal anomalies                                                              | *Counter-Clock World* (P.K. Dick), *The Time Machine* (H.G. Wells)                                                     |
| `Fiction/Science Fiction/Uchronia`    | Alternate history                                                                                       | *The Man in the High Castle* (P.K. Dick)                                                                               |
| `Fiction/Thriller/Action`             | Focus on chases, physical plots. The protagonist is threatened                                          | *The Great Train Robbery* (Michael Crichton)                                                                           |
| `Fiction/Thriller/Politics`           | Focus on power intrigues and espionage                                                                  | *American Tabloid* (James Ellroy)                                                                                      |
| `Fiction/Thriller/Psychology`         | Focus on the mind of the killer or the victim                                                           | *Almost Blue* (Carlo Lucarelli), *I Married a Dead Man* (Cornell Woolrich)                                             |
