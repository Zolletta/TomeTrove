# The three universal rules

Every classification in the system obeys three rules. They are concise; the rationale for each follows.

## 1. Autonomy

Library systems group disciplines under umbrella prefixes (`Science/Physics`, `Art/Painting`). TomeTrove drops the umbrella: `Physics` and `Painting` stand on their own. This keeps paths short and treats every discipline as a first-class browse target. `Science` survives only as a catch-all for general-science works that span disciplines.

## 2. Substantivization

**Every noun is singular.** "Italian Literature" becomes `Literature/Italy` — the adjective "Italian" is recast as a sector noun (`Italy`) at the next level. "Classical Music" becomes `Music/Classical`. "Cats/Behavior" becomes `Cat/Behavior`. This rule keeps every level a single, singular noun, which makes the hierarchy regular and translatable: each level is one term, not a phrase.

## 3. Instrumental disambiguation

A Shakespeare play script has Type `Theatre`. A critical essay *on* Shakespeare has Type `Essay`. Both share the genre `Theatre`, but the Type separates the work from its study. This avoids duplicating the genre tree into "creative" and "critical" branches.
